Does Rehab Actually Work? What the Data Really Shows

By RehabLookup Editorial 2026-04-06 12 min min read
Skeptical about rehab? Here's what the research says about treatment effectiveness, which programs have the highest success rates, and what makes the difference.

It's the question everyone asks but few answer honestly: does rehab actually work? The answer is nuanced — and more hopeful than the skeptics suggest. Here's what decades of research actually show about addiction treatment effectiveness.

The short answer: Yes, but with caveats

Addiction treatment works about as well as treatments for other chronic diseases. The relapse rate for substance use disorders (40-60%) is comparable to relapse rates for Type 2 diabetes (30-50%) and hypertension (50-70%). Nobody says diabetes treatment 'doesn't work' because patients sometimes relapse — the same logic applies to addiction.

What the numbers actually show

  • 40-60% of people who complete treatment maintain sobriety at one year
  • People who complete treatment are 7x more likely to achieve sustained recovery than those who don't seek treatment
  • 90-day programs produce 2x better outcomes than 30-day programs
  • Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT) for opioid use disorder reduces overdose death by 50%
  • Aftercare participation (therapy, support groups, sober living) doubles long-term success rates

Why some people say rehab doesn't work

Most negative perceptions come from two sources: unrealistic expectations (expecting a 30-day 'cure' for a chronic condition) and incomplete treatment (leaving early, skipping aftercare, not addressing co-occurring disorders). Treatment isn't a single event — it's the beginning of ongoing recovery management.

What predicts success in rehab

  • Treatment length: 90+ days significantly outperforms 28-30 days
  • Aftercare engagement: continuing therapy and support groups post-discharge
  • Family involvement: programs with family therapy components show 20-30% better outcomes
  • Dual diagnosis treatment: addressing mental health alongside addiction
  • Medication management: MAT for opioid and alcohol use disorders
  • Sober living: 90+ days in structured housing after residential treatment

Choosing a program that maximizes your odds

Not all treatment is equal. Evidence-based programs using CBT, DBT, motivational interviewing, and MAT consistently outperform programs relying solely on the 12-step model. Look for programs accredited by CARF or Joint Commission, with licensed clinical staff and published outcome data.

The biggest predictor of treatment success isn't the facility — it's the length of engagement. Every additional week in treatment measurably improves long-term outcomes. If you can do 90 days instead of 30, do 90 days.

Frequently asked questions

What if I've been to rehab before and relapsed?

Multiple treatment episodes are normal for chronic conditions. Each treatment attempt builds recovery capital — skills, insights, and neural pathways that make the next attempt more likely to succeed. Research shows that most people who achieve sustained recovery have been through 2-3 treatment episodes.
