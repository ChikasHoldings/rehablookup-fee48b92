How to Get a Medical Visa for Addiction Treatment in the United States

By Immigration Specialist February 13, 2026 10 min read min read
Coming to America for rehab? Here's your complete guide to visa requirements, documentation, and the application process.

Traveling to the United States for addiction treatment requires proper visa documentation. While the process may seem daunting, thousands of international patients successfully navigate it each year. Understanding the requirements and preparing thoroughly makes approval far more likely.

The B-2 visitor visa is the standard option for medical treatment in America. While commonly known as a "tourist visa," the B-2 category explicitly includes "medical treatment" as a permitted purpose. This visa allows stays up to six months, with possible extensions.

Visa Waiver Program (VWP) countries present a simpler option. Citizens of 40 countries (including most of Western Europe, Japan, Australia, and others) can enter the US for medical treatment under ESTA authorization without obtaining a traditional visa. VWP stays are limited to 90 days with no extensions.

Documentation requirements for medical visas include: passport valid for at least six months beyond intended stay, completed DS-160 application form, passport-sized photograph, treatment facility acceptance letter, evidence of financial ability to pay for treatment and stay, and proof of ties to your home country.

The facility acceptance letter should specifically describe your diagnosis, recommended treatment, expected duration, and estimated costs. This letter helps establish the legitimate medical purpose of your visit.

Financial documentation proves you can cover treatment costs and won't become a public charge. Include bank statements, employment verification, property documentation, or sponsor letters with supporting financial information.

Ties to your home country convince officers you'll return after treatment. Employment letters, property ownership, family obligations, business interests, and return tickets all help establish intent to depart.

The interview at a US embassy or consulate may feel intimidating but is typically straightforward. Be honest about your medical condition and treatment plans. Addiction is a recognized medical condition—approaching it matter-of-factly demonstrates seriousness about recovery.

Disclosure of prior substance use concerns many applicants. While drug addiction itself doesn't automatically disqualify visa applicants, certain drug trafficking convictions or admissions do create inadmissibility issues. Consult an immigration attorney if you have relevant criminal history.

Processing times vary dramatically by country and season. Some consulates process medical visas within weeks; others have backlogs of months. Begin the process as soon as you've selected a treatment facility.

Extensions are possible if treatment requires longer than initially planned. File Form I-539 before your authorized stay expires, including updated documentation from your treatment provider explaining medical necessity for extension.

Travel during COVID recovery may involve additional health documentation depending on current requirements. Check both US entry requirements and your country's exit/return policies.

Working with your treatment center simplifies many logistics. International-experienced facilities provide appropriate documentation, communicate directly with visa applicants, and may have relationships with medical travel agencies who specialize in these arrangements.

Denied applications can often be resubmitted with additional documentation. Common denial reasons include insufficient ties to home country, inadequate financial documentation, or incomplete medical justification. Address the specific concern raised.
