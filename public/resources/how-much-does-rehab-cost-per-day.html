How Much Does Rehab Cost Per Day? Complete 2026 Pricing Guide

By RehabLookup Editorial Team February 7, 2026 10 min read min read
Understand addiction treatment costs by level of care, from detox to outpatient, and learn strategies for affording the care you need.

Understanding the cost of addiction treatment helps you plan appropriately and evaluate your options. Rehab costs vary significantly based on the level of care, facility type, location, and amenities. This guide breaks down typical daily costs for each treatment level.

Medical Detox Costs

Medical detoxification typically costs $250-$800 per day, depending on the facility and required level of medical care. Hospital-based detox tends to be more expensive than standalone detox centers. A typical 5-7 day detox program costs $1,500-$5,000 or more without insurance.

Residential Treatment Costs

Inpatient residential treatment costs range from $300-$1,000+ per day. Standard residential programs: $300-$600 per day. Upscale or executive programs: $500-$800 per day. Luxury residential facilities: $1,000-$2,500+ per day. A 30-day residential stay typically costs $9,000-$30,000, while 60-90 day programs range from $18,000-$90,000.

Partial Hospitalization (PHP) Costs

PHP programs typically cost $350-$800 per day. Since PHP is a day program (no overnight stay), total costs are often lower than residential care despite similar clinical intensity. A 3-4 week PHP program might cost $7,000-$20,000.

Intensive Outpatient (IOP) Costs

IOP costs range from $100-$300 per session, with programs typically meeting 3-5 times per week. An 8-12 week IOP program might cost $3,000-$15,000 total. This makes IOP one of the more affordable intensive treatment options.

Standard Outpatient Therapy Costs

Individual therapy sessions typically cost $100-$250 per session. Group therapy is less expensive, often $30-$80 per session. Medication management appointments range from $100-$300.

What Affects Costs?

Several factors influence treatment costs: geographic location (major cities and coastal areas are more expensive), facility amenities and accommodations, staff credentials and staff-to-patient ratios, specialized programming (trauma, dual diagnosis, etc.), length of stay, and private vs. shared accommodations.

Making Treatment Affordable

Do not let cost deter you from seeking help. Options include: using insurance benefits (most plans cover addiction treatment), asking about sliding-scale fees, exploring state-funded treatment programs, inquiring about payment plans, checking if your employer offers EAP benefits, and considering facilities in lower-cost geographic areas.
