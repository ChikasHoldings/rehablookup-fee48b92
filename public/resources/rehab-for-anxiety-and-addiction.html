Rehab for Anxiety and Addiction: Treating Both at Once

By RehabLookup Editorial 2026-04-06 10 min min read
Anxiety and addiction fuel each other in a vicious cycle. Learn why integrated dual diagnosis treatment is essential and how to find programs that treat both conditions.

If you drink to calm your nerves, take pills to stop panic attacks, or use drugs to escape constant worry, you're not alone. Anxiety disorders and substance use disorders co-occur in nearly 40% of people seeking addiction treatment. Treating one without the other almost guarantees relapse.

How anxiety and addiction feed each other

The cycle is predictable: anxiety creates unbearable distress → substances provide temporary relief → the brain becomes dependent on substances for calm → withdrawal increases anxiety → more substance use is needed. Breaking this cycle requires treating both the anxiety disorder and the addiction simultaneously.

  • Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD): constant worry, muscle tension, sleep disruption
  • Social Anxiety: drinking or using before social situations to cope
  • Panic Disorder: substance use to prevent or stop panic attacks
  • PTSD: self-medicating trauma responses with alcohol or drugs
  • OCD: using substances to quiet obsessive thoughts

Why standard rehab often fails for anxiety-driven addiction

Programs that focus only on addiction leave the underlying anxiety untreated. When the coping mechanism (substances) is removed without addressing the root cause (anxiety), the person is left with raw, unmanaged distress. This is why anxiety-driven relapse rates are among the highest in addiction treatment.

What to look for in a dual diagnosis program

  • Licensed psychiatrist on staff (not just a therapist)
  • Evidence-based anxiety treatments: CBT, EMDR, exposure therapy
  • Medication management: SSRIs, buspirone, or non-addictive anxiolytics
  • Integrated treatment plans that address both conditions simultaneously
  • Mindfulness and somatic experiencing for anxiety management
  • Gradual exposure therapy for social anxiety and panic disorders
Benzodiazepines (Xanax, Klonopin, Ativan) are commonly prescribed for anxiety but are highly addictive. If you're in recovery, ask your psychiatrist about non-addictive alternatives like buspirone, hydroxyzine, or gabapentin.

Frequently asked questions

Will my anxiety get worse in rehab?

Initially, yes — removing substances removes your coping mechanism. But within 2-3 weeks of proper dual diagnosis treatment, most patients report significant anxiety reduction as their brain chemistry stabilizes and they learn evidence-based coping strategies.
