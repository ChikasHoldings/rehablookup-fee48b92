Rehab Success Rates by Program Type: What the Data Shows About Treatment Effectiveness

By RehabLookup Editorial Team April 4, 2026 10 min read min read
Data-driven analysis of rehab success rates by program type, length, and treatment approach with insights on what factors predict long-term sobriety.

Understanding rehab success rates helps set realistic expectations and choose the most effective treatment approach. The data shows that treatment works, but outcomes vary significantly based on program type, length, and aftercare involvement.

Overall rehab success rates

According to NIDA, approximately 40 to 60 percent of people relapse after treatment, which is comparable to relapse rates for other chronic conditions like hypertension and asthma. This means that 40 to 60 percent of people maintain sobriety after treatment — a significant success rate for a chronic condition.

Success rates by program type

  • Inpatient residential (90 days): 50 to 70 percent abstinence at 1 year
  • Inpatient residential (30 days): 30 to 50 percent abstinence at 1 year
  • Intensive outpatient (IOP): 35 to 55 percent abstinence at 1 year
  • Medication-assisted treatment (MAT): 60 to 80 percent retention and reduced use
  • 12-step programs (AA/NA): 22 to 37 percent sustained abstinence at 16 years

What factors predict success?

  • Length of treatment (90+ days significantly improves outcomes)
  • Completion of the full program
  • Active participation in aftercare
  • Strong social support network
  • Stable housing and employment after treatment
  • Treatment of co-occurring mental health conditions
  • Use of medication-assisted treatment when appropriate
  • Personal motivation and readiness for change

Why program length matters

NIDA research consistently shows that treatment lasting less than 90 days has limited effectiveness. The brain needs time to heal and new neural pathways need time to strengthen. Programs lasting 90 days or longer produce substantially better long-term outcomes across all treatment types.

The importance of aftercare

The first year after treatment is the highest-risk period for relapse. People who participate in ongoing aftercare — support groups, individual therapy, sober living, or continuing care programs — have significantly better outcomes than those who do not.

Treatment completion is the single strongest predictor of long-term success. People who finish their full treatment program are 3 to 5 times more likely to maintain sobriety than those who leave early.

Frequently asked questions

Does rehab really work?

Yes. Research consistently shows that treatment reduces substance use, improves health outcomes, decreases criminal behavior, and increases employment. Success rates are comparable to treatment for other chronic diseases.

What is the most effective type of rehab?

The most effective approach combines multiple elements: medically managed detox when needed, individual and group therapy, medication-assisted treatment when appropriate, and comprehensive aftercare planning.
