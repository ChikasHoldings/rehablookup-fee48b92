What Happens After Rehab? Your First 90 Days of Recovery

By RehabLookup Editorial 2026-04-06 11 min min read
The first 90 days after leaving rehab are the highest-risk period for relapse. Here's exactly what to expect and how to build a recovery routine that lasts.

Completing rehab is a major achievement — but it's the beginning of recovery, not the end. The first 90 days after discharge are statistically the most vulnerable period for relapse. Understanding what to expect and having a concrete plan dramatically improves your odds of sustained sobriety.

Week 1: The re-entry shock

The first week home is disorienting. You've been in a structured, substance-free environment for weeks or months. Now you're back in the world where triggers are everywhere — the liquor store on your commute, the friends who still use, the stress that drove your addiction in the first place.

  • Attend a support meeting within 24 hours of discharge
  • Remove all substances from your home before returning
  • Notify your aftercare counselor that you're home
  • Establish a daily routine immediately — structure prevents drift
  • Avoid major life decisions for the first 30 days

Days 8-30: Building the routine

By the second week, the initial adrenaline fades and reality sets in. This is when boredom, loneliness, and old thought patterns become dangerous. The antidote is a recovery routine that fills the hours your addiction used to occupy.

  • Attend 90 meetings in 90 days (AA, NA, SMART Recovery, or Refuge Recovery)
  • Continue outpatient therapy — weekly individual sessions minimum
  • Exercise 30+ minutes daily — it reduces cravings by up to 50%
  • Rebuild sleep hygiene: consistent bedtime, no screens before sleep
  • Connect with a sponsor or recovery mentor within the first two weeks

Days 31-90: Testing your foundation

The second and third months are where your recovery gets tested. The 'pink cloud' of early sobriety fades. You may face your first major trigger — a holiday, a conflict, a bad day at work. Having coping strategies rehearsed before these moments arrive is essential.

Sober living: The bridge between rehab and independence

Sober living homes provide structured, substance-free housing with built-in accountability. Residents who spend 90+ days in sober living after rehab have significantly higher long-term sobriety rates. It's the single most evidence-backed aftercare decision you can make.

Relapse is not failure — it's a signal that your recovery plan needs adjustment. If you relapse, contact your treatment provider immediately. Early intervention after relapse dramatically improves outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

When should I go back to work after rehab?

Most counselors recommend waiting 2-4 weeks after discharge before returning to full-time work. If your workplace was a trigger environment, discuss a phased return with your therapist. FMLA provides up to 12 weeks of job-protected leave.
