Signs of Drug Addiction in a Loved One: What to Look For and How to Help

By RehabLookup Editorial Team April 4, 2026 10 min read min read
Learn the physical, behavioral, and emotional warning signs that a family member or friend may be struggling with drug addiction.

Addiction often develops gradually, making it difficult for families to recognize until serious problems have already emerged. Knowing the early warning signs can help you intervene sooner.

Physical warning signs

  • Sudden weight loss or gain
  • Bloodshot or glazed eyes, unusual pupil size
  • Changes in sleep patterns
  • Deterioration in personal hygiene
  • Unexplained marks or bruises
  • Frequent nosebleeds
  • Slurred speech or impaired coordination
  • Unusual body odor or chemical smell

Behavioral warning signs

  • Withdrawal from family and social activities
  • New friend group with refusal to introduce them
  • Missing money or valuables from the home
  • Declining performance at work or school
  • Secretive behavior and locked doors
  • Loss of interest in hobbies
  • Legal problems: DUIs or arrests

Emotional and psychological signs

  • Dramatic mood swings
  • Increased irritability or anger
  • Paranoia or suspiciousness
  • Depression or lack of motivation
  • Defensiveness when questioned
  • Minimizing or denying substance use

Substance-specific signs

Different substances produce different signs. Opioids cause pinpoint pupils and drowsiness. Stimulants cause dilated pupils and hyperactivity. Marijuana produces red eyes and lethargy. Alcohol abuse leads to morning shaking and hidden bottles.

What to do if you see these signs

  • Document specific behaviors with dates
  • Have a calm, private conversation
  • Avoid blaming language
  • Research treatment options beforehand
  • Consider a professional interventionist
  • Set clear boundaries
  • Seek support through Al-Anon or family counseling
If your loved one is in immediate danger — overdose, suicidal ideation, or severe withdrawal — call 911 immediately.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if its addiction or experimentation?

The key difference is control. Experimentation involves occasional use without consequences. Addiction involves compulsive use despite negative consequences and inability to stop.
