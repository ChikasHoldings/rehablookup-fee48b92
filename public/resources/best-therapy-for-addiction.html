Best Types of Therapy for Addiction: Evidence-Based Approaches Compared

By RehabLookup Editorial Team April 4, 2026 10 min read min read
Compare the most effective evidence-based therapies for addiction treatment including CBT, DBT, EMDR, and motivational interviewing.

Not all therapy approaches are equal when it comes to addiction treatment. Research has identified several evidence-based therapies that consistently produce better outcomes. Understanding your options helps you choose a program that uses the most effective methods.

Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)

CBT is the most widely researched and validated therapy for addiction. It teaches you to identify and change negative thought patterns that drive substance use. CBT helps you develop coping skills, manage triggers, and prevent relapse. Most rehab programs include CBT as a core component.

Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT)

Originally developed for borderline personality disorder, DBT is highly effective for addiction, especially when co-occurring with emotional regulation difficulties. DBT teaches mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness.

Motivational interviewing (MI)

MI is a collaborative approach that strengthens your own motivation and commitment to change. Rather than telling you what to do, MI helps you explore your ambivalence about quitting and find your own reasons for recovery.

Contingency management (CM)

CM uses positive reinforcement to reward abstinence and healthy behaviors. Participants receive incentives like vouchers or prizes for clean drug tests. Research shows CM is particularly effective for stimulant addiction.

Eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR)

EMDR is increasingly used in addiction treatment, especially for people with trauma histories. Since trauma and addiction frequently co-occur, addressing traumatic memories through EMDR can reduce the emotional triggers that drive substance use.

Group therapy

Group therapy provides peer support, accountability, and the opportunity to learn from others experiences. Most rehab programs include process groups, psychoeducation groups, and skill-building groups.

How to choose the right therapy

  • Ask programs which evidence-based therapies they use
  • Look for programs that offer individualized treatment plans
  • Consider your specific needs: trauma, co-occurring mental health, relationship issues
  • Verify that therapists are licensed and credentialed
  • The best programs use multiple therapeutic approaches

Frequently asked questions

Is CBT or DBT better for addiction?

Both are effective. CBT focuses on changing thought patterns while DBT emphasizes emotional regulation. If you struggle with intense emotions or have a dual diagnosis, DBT may be particularly beneficial.

How long does therapy take to work?

Research suggests that at least 90 days of treatment produces the best outcomes. However, many people notice improvements in the first few weeks. Ongoing therapy after initial treatment supports long-term recovery.
