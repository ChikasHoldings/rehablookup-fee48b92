How Much Does Rehab Cost in 2026? A Complete Pricing Breakdown

By RehabLookup Editorial Team April 4, 2026 11 min read min read
Complete breakdown of addiction treatment costs in 2026, including detox, inpatient, outpatient, and luxury rehab pricing with insurance guidance.

The cost of rehab is one of the most common questions people ask when considering treatment. The reality is that costs vary enormously depending on the type of program, location, amenities, and length of stay. This guide breaks down realistic costs for every level of care in 2026.

Average costs by treatment type

  • Medical detox: $600 to $2,000 per day (3 to 7 day programs)
  • Inpatient residential (standard): $5,000 to $30,000 for 30 days
  • Inpatient residential (luxury): $30,000 to $100,000+ for 30 days
  • Partial hospitalization (PHP): $350 to $900 per day
  • Intensive outpatient (IOP): $3,000 to $10,000 total program
  • Standard outpatient therapy: $100 to $300 per session
  • Medication-assisted treatment (MAT): $200 to $500 per month

Factors that affect cost

  • Location: coastal and urban programs tend to cost more
  • Program length: 60 and 90-day programs cost proportionally more
  • Amenities: luxury facilities with private rooms, pools, and gourmet meals cost significantly more
  • Staff-to-patient ratio: more individualized attention means higher costs
  • Dual diagnosis treatment: programs treating co-occurring mental health conditions may charge more
  • Detox inclusion: programs that include medical detox charge more than those that do not

How insurance reduces your cost

Under the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act, most insurance plans must cover addiction treatment at the same level as other medical conditions. The average out-of-pocket cost with insurance is $1,000 to $5,000, compared to $10,000 to $30,000 without insurance.

Always verify your coverage before entering treatment. Call your insurance company and ask about your specific benefits for substance use disorder treatment, including deductibles, copays, and authorization requirements.

Ways to pay without insurance

  • State-funded programs (free or low-cost based on income)
  • Sliding-scale fee programs
  • SAMHSA block grant-funded facilities
  • Payment plans offered by treatment centers
  • Scholarships and grants from foundations
  • Crowdfunding
  • Healthcare credit cards (CareCredit)

Frequently asked questions

Is expensive rehab better than affordable rehab?

Not necessarily. Research shows that program quality, treatment approach, and length of stay matter more than cost. Many affordable programs produce excellent outcomes.

How much does a 90-day rehab program cost?

A 90-day inpatient program typically costs $15,000 to $90,000 depending on the facility. With insurance, out-of-pocket costs may be $3,000 to $15,000.
