Relapse Prevention Plan Template: A Practical Guide You Can Use Today

By RehabLookup Editorial Team 2026-03-09 11 min read min read
Learn how to build a relapse prevention plan that is specific, realistic, and easy to follow, including trigger mapping, coping skills, emergency steps, and a printable checklist.

Relapse is common in recovery, but it is not inevitable. A relapse prevention plan is a simple document you build ahead of time so you are not improvising when cravings, stress, or old patterns show up. This guide gives you a practical template you can tailor to your life.

What a relapse prevention plan is (and what it is not)

A relapse prevention plan is a set of clear actions you will take when risk rises. It includes your early warning signs, your most likely triggers, and a short list of steps that have a high chance of working for you. It is not a promise to never struggle, and it is not a replacement for treatment, therapy, or support.

Step 1: Define your personal relapse warning signs

Warning signs are the small changes that appear before a slip. They are usually emotional, behavioral, or social. The earlier you spot them, the easier it is to respond.

  • Sleep changes (insomnia, oversleeping, nightmares)
  • Isolation or skipping support meetings
  • Increased irritability, shame, or anxiety
  • Romanticizing past substance use
  • Stopping healthy routines (meals, movement, hygiene)
  • Hiding thoughts, lying, or minimizing problems

Step 2: Map your triggers by category

Triggers are situations, feelings, or cues that raise craving or impulsivity. When you name them, you can build specific counter-moves. Use these categories to avoid a vague plan like handle stress better.

External triggers

  • Specific places (old neighborhood, certain bars, a dealer’s area)
  • People (friends who still use, certain family dynamics)
  • Money in hand (payday, cash withdrawals)
  • Time blocks (late nights, weekends, holidays)
  • Social media content that glamorizes use

Internal triggers

  • Loneliness, boredom, or grief
  • Anger and resentment
  • Anxiety and panic symptoms
  • Shame spirals and self-criticism
  • Overconfidence (thinking you are cured)

Step 3: Choose coping skills that match the moment

Coping skills work best when they fit the intensity of the craving. Build a short menu you actually use. If your list is too long, you will not remember it under stress.

  • Low intensity: drink water, eat, take a shower, 10-minute walk
  • Medium intensity: call a sponsor, attend a meeting, journal for 15 minutes
  • High intensity: go to a safe place, ask someone to stay with you, remove access to money or transportation
  • If you use therapy skills: grounding, urge surfing, DBT distress tolerance

Step 4: Build your If-Then action plan

Turn risk into scripts. Scripts reduce decision fatigue and delay. Keep each action specific and time-bound.

  • If I feel cravings after an argument, then I will leave the room, take 10 slow breaths, and call my support person within 5 minutes.
  • If I start skipping meetings, then I will text my sponsor the same day and schedule the next two meetings.
  • If I drive past a high-risk location, then I will change route immediately and play my recovery playlist.
  • If I have cash from payday, then I will move it into a separate account and set a small weekly cash limit.

Step 5: Create an emergency plan for a slip

A slip does not have to become a full relapse. Decide what you will do within the first hour and the first 24 hours. Make it simple.

  • Stop using immediately and leave the environment
  • Call your support person and tell the truth
  • Dispose of substances and paraphernalia safely
  • If overdose risk exists, do not stay alone; call emergency services if needed
  • Schedule next-step care: urgent appointment, higher level of care, or detox if required

Relapse prevention plan template (copy and fill in)

  • My top 3 warning signs are: ________
  • My top 5 triggers are: ________
  • My 5 go-to coping skills are: ________
  • My 3 safest places are: ________
  • People I will contact (name + number): ________
  • Steps I will take in the first hour after a slip: ________
  • My next-step treatment plan if risk stays high: ________
If you want help turning this template into a plan that fits your history, diagnosis, and environment, professional treatment and ongoing recovery support can make the difference.
