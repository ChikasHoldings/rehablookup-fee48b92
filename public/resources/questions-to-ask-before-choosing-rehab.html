15 Questions to Ask Before Choosing a Rehab Center

By RehabLookup Editorial 2026-04-07 9 min read min read
The exact questions families should ask any treatment center before admission — from clinical credentials to discharge planning — to make sure you are choosing the right program.

Choosing a rehab center is one of the most important decisions a family will ever make, and most people have never done it before. The internet is full of polished marketing, and it can be hard to tell which programs deliver real results. These 15 questions cut through the noise and help you evaluate any facility based on what actually matters for recovery outcomes.

Clinical quality questions

1. What is your state license number and accreditation status?

A legitimate facility will give you the license number immediately and tell you whether they hold JCAHO, CARF, or other accreditation. If they hesitate or deflect, consider it a red flag. You can verify these on the state licensing portal and accreditor websites.

2. What are the credentials of your clinical staff?

Look for licensed professionals: psychiatrists (MD/DO), psychologists (PhD/PsyD), licensed clinical social workers (LCSW), licensed professional counselors (LPC), and certified addiction counselors (CAC). Ask about the staff-to-patient ratio — a good program has no more than 8 to 10 patients per counselor.

3. What evidence-based therapies do you use?

The gold standard includes cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), motivational interviewing (MI), contingency management, and trauma-focused therapies like EMDR. Be cautious of programs that rely exclusively on 12-step philosophy without clinical therapy components.

4. Do you offer medication-assisted treatment?

For opioid and alcohol use disorders, MAT (Suboxone, naltrexone, Vivitrol) significantly improves outcomes. Programs that refuse to offer MAT on philosophical grounds are not following current medical guidelines. A quality program will evaluate each patient individually for MAT appropriateness.

Treatment structure questions

5. What does a typical day look like?

You should hear about structured therapy sessions (individual, group, and family), psychoeducation, skill-building activities, physical wellness, and free time. Beware of programs that fill the day mostly with peer support meetings or unstructured time.

6. How do you handle co-occurring mental health conditions?

Many people with addiction also have depression, anxiety, PTSD, or bipolar disorder. A good program provides integrated dual-diagnosis treatment with a psychiatrist on staff who can manage medications. Programs that say they will 'address mental health after sobriety' are outdated.

7. What is your approach to family involvement?

Research consistently shows that family involvement improves treatment outcomes. Ask about family therapy sessions, family education programs, visiting policies, and how the facility communicates with family members. Good programs involve families early and often.

Financial and insurance questions

8. What will my total out-of-pocket cost be?

A transparent facility will verify your insurance, calculate your expected costs, and provide a written estimate before admission. If they cannot give you a cost estimate, or if they pressure you to admit before verification, find another program.

9. What happens if my insurance denies continued stay?

Good programs have experienced utilization review teams who appeal denials and advocate for continued coverage. Ask specifically: will they discharge you if insurance stops paying, or do they have options like scholarship funds, payment plans, or step-down to a less costly level of care?

Aftercare and outcomes questions

10. What is your discharge planning process?

Treatment does not end at discharge. A quality program begins discharge planning in the first week: identifying outpatient therapists, connecting with sober living, scheduling MAT prescriptions, arranging follow-up appointments, and coordinating with your support network.

11. Do you offer alumni or continuing care programs?

The first 90 days after discharge are the highest-risk period. Programs with active alumni networks, continuing care groups, and regular check-ins have better long-term outcomes. Ask what happens after you leave.

12. Do you track and share your outcomes data?

Quality programs track completion rates, readmission rates, and post-discharge outcomes. They should be willing to share this data. Programs that make vague claims about success rates without data are usually not measuring outcomes at all.

Safety and comfort questions

13. What are your policies on phones and visitors?

Every program handles this differently. Some allow limited phone use, others restrict devices entirely. Neither approach is inherently better — what matters is that the policy is clearly communicated and consistently enforced.

14. What is the patient-to-staff ratio at night?

Adequate overnight staffing is a safety issue, especially during detox. There should always be at least one awake staff member for every 10 to 15 patients, with a nurse on-site or on-call 24/7 for medical programs.

15. Can I tour the facility before admission?

Any reputable program will welcome tours or, at minimum, offer a virtual walkthrough. If a facility refuses to let you see the premises before committing, look elsewhere.
