Does Health Insurance Cover Detox? Your Coverage Rights Explained

By RehabLookup Editorial Team February 7, 2026 8 min read min read
Understand your insurance coverage for medical detoxification, including what federal law requires and how to maximize your benefits.

Medical detoxification is often the first step in addiction treatment, and understanding your insurance coverage for this crucial service can eliminate a major barrier to getting help. The good news: federal law requires most insurance plans to cover detox services.

Federal Coverage Requirements

Under the Affordable Care Act, substance abuse treatment—including detoxification—is classified as an Essential Health Benefit. This means marketplace plans, Medicaid expansion plans, and most employer-sponsored insurance must cover detox services.

The Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act further requires that coverage for addiction treatment, including detox, be comparable to coverage for other medical conditions. Insurers cannot impose stricter limitations on detox than they would for other medical emergencies.

What Detox Services Are Typically Covered

Most insurance plans cover medical supervision during withdrawal, medications to manage withdrawal symptoms, vital sign monitoring and medical interventions, assessment for continued treatment needs, and transfer to rehabilitation programs.

Understanding Your Specific Coverage

While federal law mandates coverage, the specifics vary by plan. Key factors include your deductible (what you pay before insurance covers services), coinsurance or copays (your share of each service), in-network vs. out-of-network coverage, prior authorization requirements, and length of stay limitations.

Medicare and Medicaid Detox Coverage

Medicare Part A covers inpatient detox in hospitals, while Part B covers outpatient detoxification services. Medicaid coverage varies by state but generally includes detox as a covered benefit in expansion states. Contact your state Medicaid office for specific information.

What to Do Before Detox

To ensure coverage, verify your benefits by calling your insurance company, get prior authorization if required, choose an in-network facility when possible, ask the facility about insurance verification services, and understand your potential out-of-pocket costs upfront.

If Coverage Is Denied

Insurance denials for detox can often be appealed, especially if the service is medically necessary. Request a written explanation of the denial, gather supporting documentation from medical providers, file a formal appeal, and contact your state insurance commissioner if needed.
