PHP vs IOP: Understanding Partial Hospitalization and Intensive Outpatient

By RehabLookup Editorial Team 2026-02-07 9 min read min read
Learn the key differences between Partial Hospitalization Programs (PHP) and Intensive Outpatient Programs (IOP) to choose the right treatment intensity.

PHP (Partial Hospitalization Program) and IOP (Intensive Outpatient Program) represent the two highest levels of outpatient addiction treatment. Understanding the differences helps you step down appropriately from residential care or choose the right starting point for your recovery journey.

What Is PHP (Partial Hospitalization)?

PHP provides hospital-level care without overnight stays. Treatment typically runs 5-7 days per week for 6-8 hours daily. PHP includes medical monitoring, daily psychiatrist access, multiple therapy sessions, and structured programming similar to residential treatment. Patients return home or to sober living each evening.

What Is IOP (Intensive Outpatient)?

IOP offers substantial treatment while accommodating work and life responsibilities. Programs typically meet 3-5 days per week for 3-4 hours per session, often in evenings. IOP focuses on group therapy, individual counseling, skill-building, and relapse prevention without the medical intensity of PHP.

Side-by-Side Comparison

  • Hours Per Week: PHP = 30-40 hours; IOP = 9-20 hours
  • Days Per Week: PHP = 5-7 days; IOP = 3-5 days
  • Medical Oversight: PHP = daily medical check-ins; IOP = as needed
  • Psychiatry Access: PHP = on-site daily; IOP = scheduled appointments
  • Work Compatible: PHP = difficult; IOP = yes, often evening schedules
  • Typical Duration: PHP = 2-4 weeks; IOP = 8-12 weeks
  • Step In Treatment: PHP = after residential; IOP = after PHP or direct entry

When PHP Is Appropriate

PHP is typically recommended after residential treatment, for those needing medical stabilization, individuals with co-occurring psychiatric conditions requiring daily monitoring, or situations where IOP intensity is insufficient. PHP provides structure while beginning real-world reintegration.

When IOP Is Appropriate

IOP works well after completing PHP, for moderate addiction without need for medical monitoring, individuals with stable jobs and supportive home environments, or as an alternative to residential for highly motivated people. Evening IOP programs allow continued employment.

Insurance Coverage

Both PHP and IOP are covered by most insurance plans as essential health benefits. PHP typically has higher coverage limits due to its intensity. Many people progress through both levels: residential → PHP → IOP → standard outpatient.
