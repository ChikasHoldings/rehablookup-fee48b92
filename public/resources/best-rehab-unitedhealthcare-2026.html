Best Rehab Centers That Accept UnitedHealthcare in 2026

By RehabLookup Editorial 2026-04-07 10 min read min read
How to use your UnitedHealthcare benefits for addiction treatment, which facilities are in-network, and how to navigate Optum Behavioral Health authorization.

UnitedHealthcare is the largest health insurer in the United States, covering more than 50 million Americans. If you have a UHC plan, your addiction treatment benefits are managed through Optum Behavioral Health, a subsidiary that handles all mental health and substance use disorder claims. Understanding how Optum works is the key to getting your treatment covered and minimizing out-of-pocket costs.

What UnitedHealthcare covers for addiction treatment

All UHC plans are required to cover substance use disorder treatment under the Mental Health Parity Act. Covered services include medical detoxification, inpatient residential treatment, partial hospitalization programs, intensive outpatient programs, outpatient individual and group therapy, medication-assisted treatment (Suboxone, naltrexone, Vivitrol, methadone), and telehealth addiction counseling.

UHC uses Optum's clinical criteria (based on ASAM guidelines) to determine the appropriate level of care. This means your treatment team must demonstrate that the recommended level of care meets specific medical necessity criteria.

UHC plan types and rehab coverage

  • UHC Choice Plus PPO: Best flexibility for rehab. In-network and out-of-network coverage with 70-80% in-network coverage after deductible
  • UHC Options PPO: Similar to Choice Plus with slightly higher cost-sharing. Still allows out-of-network at reduced coverage
  • UHC Navigate/Core HMO: In-network only. Must use Optum-contracted facilities. Lower premiums but limited choice
  • UHC Dual Complete (Medicare-Medicaid): Full coverage for addiction treatment with little to no cost for qualifying individuals
  • UHC Community Plan (Medicaid): State-specific Medicaid managed by UHC. Free addiction treatment coverage

Navigating Optum authorization

Optum Behavioral Health handles prior authorization for all residential and inpatient treatment under UHC plans. The treatment facility calls Optum at (866) 261-5488 to request authorization. Initial authorizations are typically 5 to 10 days for residential treatment, with concurrent reviews every 3 to 5 days thereafter.

The key to smooth authorization is choosing a facility experienced with Optum. Programs that regularly work with UHC patients know the clinical language Optum requires and can document medical necessity effectively. Facilities unfamiliar with Optum criteria often receive denials that could have been prevented.

Typical costs with UHC

In-network residential treatment typically costs $1,500 to $5,000 out of pocket after deductible for a 30-day stay. IOP copays range from $25 to $75 per session. Individual therapy copays are usually $20 to $50. Once you hit your out-of-pocket maximum (typically $8,000 to $16,000 per year), UHC covers remaining costs at 100%.

Appealing UHC/Optum denials

UHC and Optum have faced significant regulatory scrutiny for behavioral health claim denials. Several state attorneys general and the U.S. Department of Labor have investigated Optum's denial practices. If your claim is denied, you have strong grounds for appeal.

  • Request the denial letter with the specific ASAM criteria cited
  • Ask your treatment team for a peer-to-peer review with the Optum medical director
  • File a formal internal appeal within 180 days
  • If denied again, file an external review with your state insurance regulator
  • Report potential parity violations to your state insurance department or the DOL

Frequently asked questions

Does UHC cover rehab for my spouse or dependent?

Yes. All covered dependents on your UHC plan have the same substance use disorder benefits as the primary member. Privacy regulations prevent UHC from disclosing treatment details to the policyholder without the patient's consent.

Can I use UHC out of state?

Yes. UHC is a national network. You can use your benefits at in-network facilities in any state. Many people travel for treatment to access specialized programs or get distance from their home environment, and UHC covers this the same as local treatment.
