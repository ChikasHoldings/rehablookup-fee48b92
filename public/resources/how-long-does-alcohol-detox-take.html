How Long Does Alcohol Detox Take? Complete Timeline Guide

By RehabLookup Editorial 2026-04-06 9 min min read
Alcohol detox typically takes 5-10 days, but severity varies widely. Learn the hour-by-hour timeline, when symptoms peak, and why medical supervision matters.

Alcohol detox is one of the few withdrawal processes that can be life-threatening without medical supervision. Understanding the timeline helps you prepare mentally and ensures you get the right level of care. Here's what to expect hour by hour.

The alcohol detox timeline

6-12 hours after last drink

Mild symptoms begin: anxiety, insomnia, nausea, hand tremors, sweating, headache, increased heart rate. These symptoms are your body reacting to the sudden absence of alcohol's depressant effects on your nervous system.

12-24 hours

Symptoms intensify. Some people experience alcoholic hallucinosis — visual, auditory, or tactile hallucinations while remaining conscious and oriented. This is not delirium tremens (DTs) but should be monitored closely.

24-72 hours: The danger zone

This is the highest-risk window. Seizures can occur, typically within 24-48 hours. Delirium tremens (DTs) — confusion, rapid heartbeat, fever, hallucinations — can develop in 48-72 hours. DTs occur in approximately 3-5% of heavy drinkers and are a medical emergency with a 37% mortality rate if untreated.

Days 4-7: Gradual improvement

Acute withdrawal symptoms begin subsiding. Sleep gradually improves. Anxiety and irritability decrease. Medical teams typically begin tapering medications (benzodiazepines) used to manage withdrawal.

Days 7-14+: Post-acute withdrawal

Physical symptoms resolve, but post-acute withdrawal syndrome (PAWS) can persist for weeks or months: mood swings, sleep disturbances, difficulty concentrating, and intermittent cravings. PAWS is a leading cause of early relapse.

Factors that affect detox length

  • Duration and amount of drinking: decades of heavy use = longer detox
  • History of previous withdrawals: each successive detox tends to be more severe (kindling effect)
  • Overall health: liver function, nutrition status, co-occurring conditions
  • Age: older adults typically experience more severe withdrawal
  • Polydrug use: concurrent benzodiazepine or opioid dependence complicates detox
Never attempt alcohol detox alone if you've been drinking heavily for months or years. Alcohol withdrawal seizures and delirium tremens can be fatal. Medical detox facilities monitor vital signs 24/7 and use medications to prevent life-threatening complications.

Frequently asked questions

Can I detox from alcohol at home?

Only if your drinking has been mild (a few drinks daily for a short period) and you have no history of seizures or DTs. For heavy, long-term drinkers, medical detox is medically necessary and potentially life-saving. When in doubt, get a professional assessment.
