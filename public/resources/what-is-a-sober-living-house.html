What Is a Sober Living House? How It Works, Costs, and How to Find One

By RehabLookup Editorial Team April 4, 2026 9 min read min read
Understand what sober living houses are, how they work, what they cost, and how they help people transition from rehab to independent living.

Sober living houses are structured, substance-free residences that serve as a bridge between inpatient rehab and independent living. They provide a stable, supportive environment where residents can practice recovery skills while gradually reintegrating into daily life.

How sober living works

Residents live together in a shared home, following house rules that typically include mandatory sobriety, participation in house meetings, and contribution to chores. Most sober living homes require residents to work or attend school, attend recovery meetings, and submit to random drug testing.

House rules and expectations

  • Complete abstinence from drugs and alcohol
  • Random drug and alcohol testing
  • Attendance at weekly house meetings
  • Participation in 12-step or other recovery meetings
  • Curfew compliance
  • Financial responsibility (rent and shared expenses)
  • Chore rotation and house maintenance
  • No overnight guests without approval

Levels of sober living

NARR (National Alliance for Recovery Residences) defines four levels. Level 1 is peer-run with minimal structure. Level 2 includes a house manager and more accountability. Level 3 adds licensed clinical services. Level 4 is essentially a residential treatment center.

Cost of sober living

Monthly costs range from $500 to $2,500 depending on location, amenities, and level of support. Some programs accept insurance or offer scholarships. State-funded sober living options exist in many areas.

Benefits of sober living after rehab

  • Structured environment reduces relapse risk
  • Peer support from others in recovery
  • Gradual transition to independence
  • Accountability through drug testing and house rules
  • Practice life skills in a safe setting
  • Continued access to recovery resources
Research shows that residents who stay in sober living for 90 days or longer have significantly better long-term sobriety rates compared to those who return home directly after rehab.

How to find a good sober living home

  • Look for NARR-certified residences
  • Visit the home in person before committing
  • Ask about drug testing policies and consequences
  • Check staff credentials and house manager experience
  • Read reviews from former residents
  • Verify the home is licensed if required by your state

Frequently asked questions

How long do people stay in sober living?

Average stays range from 3 to 12 months. Some residents stay longer, especially if they are rebuilding employment or housing stability. There is no set maximum in most homes.

Can I work while in sober living?

Yes. Most sober living homes require residents to work or attend school. Employment helps build routine, financial stability, and self-esteem during recovery.
