Mindfulness and Meditation in Addiction Recovery

By RehabLookup Team February 2025 9 min read min read
How mindfulness practices support recovery and help manage cravings and stress.

Mindfulness and meditation are increasingly recognized as valuable tools in addiction recovery. These practices help manage cravings, reduce stress, and build awareness.

What Is Mindfulness?

Mindfulness means paying attention to the present moment without judgment. It's about observing thoughts and feelings without automatically reacting to them.

How Mindfulness Helps Recovery

Mindfulness creates space between triggers and responses. Instead of automatically reaching for substances, you learn to observe cravings, recognize them as temporary, and choose healthy responses.

Evidence for Mindfulness

Research shows mindfulness-based interventions reduce relapse risk, decrease craving intensity, improve emotional regulation, and reduce stress and anxiety.

Simple Mindfulness Practices

Start with basic exercises like mindful breathing (focusing on breath for a few minutes), body scan meditation, mindful eating, and walking meditation.

Meditation in Treatment Programs

Many treatment programs now incorporate mindfulness training. Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention (MBRP) is an evidence-based program specifically designed for recovery.

Apps and Resources

Many apps offer guided meditations for recovery. Insight Timer, Headspace, and Calm all have addiction-specific content. Recovery dharma and refuge recovery groups incorporate meditation.

Building a Practice

Start with just five minutes daily. Consistency matters more than duration. Over time, mindfulness becomes a natural response to stress and cravings.
