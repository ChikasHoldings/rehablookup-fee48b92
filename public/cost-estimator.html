Understanding Treatment Costs

The cost of addiction treatment varies significantly based on the type of program, length of stay, amenities, and location. Most importantly, your insurance coverage can dramatically reduce out-of-pocket expenses.

Don't let cost concerns delay treatment. Many facilities offer payment plans, and financial assistance options exist for those who qualify. The cost of untreated addiction—in healthcare, lost wages, and quality of life—far exceeds the investment in recovery.

Typical Treatment Costs (Without Insurance)

Medical Detox

$1,000 - $3,000
5-10 days typical stay

Medically supervised withdrawal management with 24/7 monitoring. Costs depend on substances and medical complexity.

  • Hospital-based: $1,500-$3,000/day
  • Standalone detox: $500-$1,500/day
  • Includes medications & monitoring

Inpatient / Residential

$10,000 - $50,000+
30-90 days typical stay

24-hour structured care with therapy, housing, and meals. Wide range based on amenities and program quality.

  • Standard programs: $10,000-$25,000/month
  • Executive/luxury: $50,000-$100,000+/month
  • State-funded options available

Partial Hospitalization (PHP)

$7,000 - $15,000
4-8 weeks typical duration

Intensive day treatment (5-7 hours daily) without overnight stay. Often used as step-down from residential.

  • $350-$600 per day typical
  • 5-7 days per week
  • Housing costs additional

Intensive Outpatient (IOP)

$3,000 - $10,000
8-12 weeks typical duration

Flexible treatment (3-4 hours, 3-5 days/week) allowing work and family responsibilities to continue.

  • $250-$400 per session typical
  • 9-15 hours per week
  • Most insurance-friendly option

💡 With Insurance, Costs Drop Significantly

Most people with health insurance pay only 10-30% of these costs out-of-pocket after deductibles and copays. Our free insurance verification service can tell you exactly what your plan covers before you commit to any program.

Factors That Affect Treatment Cost

Program Type & Intensity

Residential treatment with 24-hour care costs more than outpatient programs. Medical detox adds expense due to clinical monitoring requirements.

Length of Stay

Longer programs cost more but often have better outcomes. Many facilities offer discounts for extended stays (60-90 days vs. 30 days).

Location & Amenities

Programs in high-cost areas (California, Florida) and those with luxury amenities (private rooms, spa, gourmet meals) command premium prices.

Specialized Services

Dual diagnosis treatment, trauma therapy, holistic services, and executive programs typically cost more than standard addiction-only treatment.

Insurance Coverage

In-network facilities cost less out-of-pocket. Your deductible, copay, and out-of-pocket maximum significantly impact final costs.

Additional Services

Costs may increase for individual therapy sessions, psychiatric evaluations, medications, lab work, and aftercare planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does drug rehab cost without insurance?

Without insurance, addiction treatment costs vary widely: Medical detox ranges from $1,000-$3,000 for 5-7 days. Inpatient/residential treatment costs $10,000-$50,000+ for 30 days, with luxury programs exceeding $100,000/month. Outpatient programs (IOP) cost $3,000-$10,000 for 8-12 weeks. Many facilities offer payment plans and sliding scale fees.

Does insurance cover the full cost of rehab?

Insurance typically covers a significant portion but not all costs. Most plans have deductibles ($500-$5,000), copays ($20-$100 per visit), and coinsurance (10-30% of costs). Coverage limits may apply to days in treatment. In-network facilities usually have lower out-of-pocket costs than out-of-network options.

What is the average cost of 30-day inpatient rehab?

The average 30-day inpatient program costs $15,000-$30,000 at standard facilities. Luxury and executive programs range from $50,000-$100,000+. State-funded programs may cost little to nothing for qualifying individuals. With insurance, out-of-pocket costs typically range from $1,000-$10,000 depending on your plan.
